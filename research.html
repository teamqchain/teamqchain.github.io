Cryptographic Research & Architecture

Securing the Ledger Against
Quantum Supremacy

A rigorous architectural exploration of integrating lattice-based Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) into Hyperledger Fabric. Transitioning from vulnerable ECDSA and RSA to NIST-finalized FIPS standards.

System Flow Topology Algorithm Benchmark

Core Findings & Thesis

  • ■ The Threat: Shor's algorithm renders classical ECDSA and RSA breakable on quantum computers, allowing forgery of blockchain identity MSPs.
  • ■ The Standard: Migration to NIST finalists: ML-DSA (Dilithium) for signatures and ML-KEM (Kyber) for envelope encryption.
  • ■ The Tradeoff: PQC signatures are ~40x–100x larger than ECDSA, introducing potential consensus serialization bottlenecks.
  • ■ The Solution: Hybrid architecture storing minimal salted hash commitments on-chain while encrypted payloads reside on IPFS.

The Quantum Threat Horizon

Blockchain immutability is permanent: encrypted transactions captured today can be decrypted tomorrow. While symmetric hashing (SHA3-256) remains resistant, the asymmetric foundation of Fabric identities is critically exposed.

Cryptographic Vulnerability Matrix

ECDSA / RSA-2048

CRITICAL RISK

Polynomial-time factorization via Shor's algorithm. Allows total forgery of MSP admin certificates and Fabric gateway transaction signatures.

SHA-256 / SHA3-256

MODERATE (SECURE AT 256-BIT)

Square-root speedup via Grover's algorithm effectively halves entropy. 256-bit hashes provide a full 128 bits of post-quantum security, remaining resilient.

Lattice-Based (ML-DSA / ML-KEM)

POST-QUANTUM SECURE

NIST finalized standards (FIPS 204 & 203). Hardness proven on shortest vector problems (SVP) in high-dimensional lattices with no known sub-exponential quantum attacks.

The Ledger Bloat Dilemma

Post-quantum security involves an inherent performance and storage cost. The primary challenges are Public Key & Signature Sizes and Throughput Overhead.

Throughput Impact (TPS Reduction)

Due to cryptographic key verification overhead and network packet serialization, transitioning from classical ECDSA to hybrid PQC results in an estimated 50–60% throughput drop without hardware acceleration.
